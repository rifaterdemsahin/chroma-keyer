Automated Chroma Keying with HTML5 Canvas

This project implements an automated, browser-native green screen removal tool. It processes video footage in real-time, removing the green background without the need for complex, manual human intervention typically required in traditional NLE software.

Process & Steps Followed

  1. Setup Video and Canvas: Loaded the video footage (2025-09-28-07-57.mp4) and a dynamically linked background image (background.png) into the Document Object Model (DOM).
  2. Divide and Conquer: Instead of processing the entire video file at once, the problem is divided frame-by-frame.
  3. Frame Extraction: Using requestAnimationFrame, we continually draw the current video frame onto a hidden buffer canvas.
  4. Pixel Analysis (Automation): We extract the ImageData (RGBA array) from the buffer canvas. We analyze each pixel to identify the background.
  5. Automated Green Detection: If the Green channel is significantly dominant over the Red and Blue channels (using a relative threshold formula), we classify the pixel as "green screen".
  6. Background Cropping & Substitution: For every green pixel, we crop it out by replacing its RGBA values with the corresponding pixel values from the background image, merging the footage and background seamlessly.
  7. Rendering: The modified pixel data is painted onto the visible output canvas, creating the final composite video stream.

Libraries Installed/Used: No external bloated libraries were necessary. We achieved this pure automation using standard, highly optimized Web APIs (HTML5 Canvas + JavaScript ImageData). This "divide and conquer" approach keeps the application lightweight and native, eliminating the need for large Node.js modules or OpenCV payloads.

Comparison with DaVinci Resolve

DaVinci Resolve (Delta Keyer / 3D Keyer):

  • Pros: Highly advanced spill suppression, automated edge refinement, and professional-grade color math.
  • Cons: As noted, it can have drawbacks when shadows and locations change. The "green key" often requires human intervention to tweak thresholds, clean plates, and adjust garbage mattes when the lighting shifts.

Our Automated Browser-based Keyer:

  • Pros: Designed for zero human intervention. It dynamically processes relative color channels to crop the green out automatically. It runs entirely on the client side in the browser, meaning it's lightweight and instantly deployable.
  • Cons: Pure RGB thresholding can struggle with very aggressive shadows or over-exposed highlights if the green loses its dominant hue, and it lacks the complex spill-suppression of DaVinci's node-based compositing.

Limitations

  • Lighting Consistency: Extreme shadows might cause the green to become too dark, breaking the automated RGB threshold logic.
  • Subject Colors: If the subject wears green or colors with very high green values, they may become transparent.
  • Performance: CPU-based pixel manipulation in JavaScript is fast enough for standard resolutions, but for 4K 60FPS video, it may drop frames on lower-end devices. Future iterations could use WebGL shaders for GPU acceleration.
